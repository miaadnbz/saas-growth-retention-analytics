# Paid Social Onboarding Experiment

## Business objective

Increase activation and downstream retention among Paid Social trials without increasing support burden or customer dissatisfaction.

## Hypothesis

A targeted day-3 onboarding intervention focused on teammate invitation and integration connection will increase the 14-day activation rate among under-activated Paid Social trials.

## Eligible population

Paid Social trial accounts that have completed fewer than two critical onboarding milestones by the end of Day 3.

## Randomization unit

Account.

Randomizing at the account level prevents members of the same customer organization from receiving different experiences.

## Control experience

The existing onboarding journey.

## Treatment experience

* A simplified in-product onboarding checklist
* A contextual teammate-invitation prompt
* A recommended integration based on the customer’s workflow
* A day-3 lifecycle email directing the customer to the unfinished actions

## Primary metric

14-day activation rate.

Activation is defined as completing at least two of the following within 14 days:

* Create a project
* Invite a teammate
* Connect an integration

## Secondary metrics

* Trial-to-paid conversion
* Time to activation
* 30-day product engagement
* 90-day paid retention

## Guardrail metrics

* Email unsubscribe rate
* Support tickets per account
* High-priority support tickets
* Early cancellation rate
* Negative customer feedback

## Decision rule

Recommend rollout only when:

1. The activation-rate improvement is commercially meaningful.
2. The estimated effect is sufficiently precise.
3. Guardrail metrics do not materially worsen.

## Analytical limitation

The existing portfolio analysis is observational. Higher activation is associated with stronger retention, but the analysis does not establish that activation itself caused the retention difference.
